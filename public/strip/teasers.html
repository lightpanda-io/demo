First paragraph of the story. Sourdough is a bread made by the fermentation of dough using wild lactobacillaceae and yeast, which give it a mildly sour taste. The lactic acid produced by the bacteria gives it a longer shelf life than breads made with baker's yeast.

Second paragraph of the story. A starter is a stable culture of flour and water kept at room temperature and fed on a schedule; once it doubles predictably between feedings it is ready to leaven a loaf, and a spoonful of it will seed the next batch for years.

Third paragraph of the story. Bakers judge readiness by the float test, dropping a spoonful of starter into water and watching whether it rises, though a well kept starter makes the test redundant because its rhythm is already known.
