The last lighthouse keeper on the west coast

By Rhona Blair, 4 September 2026

When the light at Skerry Point was automated in 1998, Donald Ferguson was supposed to retire with it. Instead he stayed on as a caretaker, then as a volunteer, and now, at seventy-nine, as the only person who still climbs the 148 steps every evening to check that the lamp is turning.

"The machine does the work," he says, "but a machine does not notice when a gull has built a nest against the lens." He found one in April. The light had been dimmed by a third for a week before anyone on shore noticed.

The board that owns the lighthouse has tolerated the arrangement because it costs nothing. Ferguson lives in the keeper's cottage rent free and receives no wage. He is, on paper, a tenant with an unusual hobby.

That may change next year. The cottage needs a new roof and the board would rather sell it than repair it. A holiday let, they suggest, would pay for the upkeep of the tower. Ferguson has read the proposal and put it in a drawer.

"I will climb the stairs until I cannot," he says. "After that, it is their lighthouse."
